Ebisu Ramen Guide: 4 Very Different Bowls Near the Station

Last checked: October 21, 2026. Hours, menus and walking times change. Check each shop's latest information before you go.

What kind of neighborhood is Ebisu?

Ebisu is a relaxed, grown-up neighborhood just south of Shibuya. It is popular with people who live in Tokyo, including many from overseas, and it has a lot of small restaurants and bars.

  • Getting there: It is one stop from Shibuya on the JR Yamanote Line. From Shinjuku, the JR Saikyo Line is a direct option. The Hibiya Line also stops here. Check a route planner for today's times and fares.
  • Yebisu Garden Place is the area's big shopping and dining complex. The neighborhood is named after Yebisu Beer, which was brewed on this site.
  • Ebisu Yokocho is a lively alley of casual izakaya (Japanese pubs).

It is also a very good ramen neighborhood. Here are four shops, each with a different style.

1. Tsukumo Ramen, Ebisu Main Shop (cheese ramen)

Address: 1-1-36 Hiroo, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (Hongoku Building, 1st floor)
Getting there: About 7 to 8 minutes on foot from the East Exit of Ebisu Station, going straight along Meiji-dori toward Hiroo. Walking times differ by source, so use a map app.
Hours: 11:00 to 3:00 the next morning (check before you go)
Signature bowl: the "Ganso (Original) Cheese Ramen", topped with a mountain of Gouda cheese from Tokachi, Hokkaido. The shop also serves the "Ganso Tsukumo Tororo Ramen".
Ordering: You buy a ticket from a machine outside, and you can choose things like noodle thickness and the amount of back fat.

My take: I have eaten here. The cheese is piled high, and it is a very satisfying bowl. On the table there are bean sprouts and green onions that you can add as much as you like for free, so you also get the fun of customizing your bowl. I think visitors from overseas will be very surprised. I have nothing to complain about at this shop.

Cheese is a dairy product. If you avoid dairy, ask the staff first.

2. Ramen Butayama, Ebisu (Jiro-style)

Address: 4-1-18 Ebisu, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo
Getting there: About 2 to 3 minutes on foot from the East Exit of Ebisu Station.
Hours: 11:00 to 2:00 the next morning (check before you go)
Style: "Jiro-style" ramen: thick noodles, a rich pork soup, thick-cut pork (chashu) and a lot of vegetables. The menu is the "Small Ramen" and the "Ramen".

How to order (and the "Zen-mashi" bowl)

Just before your ramen is served, the staff will ask "Ninniku iremasu ka?" ("Do you want garlic?"). This is when you tell them how you want your bowl. The shop's own guide on the wall explains the options:

  • Garlic (ninniku): none (nuki), a little (sukoshi, half a tablespoon), regular (1 tablespoon) or extra (mashimashi, 2 tablespoons).
  • Vegetables (yasai): less (150 g), standard (300 g), more (450 g) or extra (600 g). The shop says the 600 g option is very large and asks first-time visitors to avoid it.
  • Back fat (abura): standard, more (abura) or extra (abura mashimashi).
  • Strength of the sauce: standard, stronger (karame) or very strong (karakara).
  • If you are happy with the standard toppings, you do not need to say anything. The guide recommends the standard size for first-timers.

The shop's guide also lists "Zen-mashi" (全マシ): garlic, vegetables at 450 g, extra back fat and a stronger sauce. That is the bowl in the photo below. The toppings are free, so I recommend trying it once.

My take: I have eaten here. I think most visitors have never eaten a bowl like this, and it may be a real shock. Be careful when you order: even the "Small Ramen" is the size of a large bowl at an ordinary ramen shop. Only ask for the extra toppings if you are sure you can finish them.

3. Teuchi Oya-dori Chukasoba Ayagawa (chicken broth, hand-cut noodles)

Address: 1-21-18 Ebisu, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (Lights Ebisu building, 1st floor)
Getting there: About 5 to 6 minutes on foot from the East Exit of Ebisu Station.
Hours: 11:00 to 21:30. It may close early when the noodles sell out.
Style: The signature bowl is the "Oya-dori Chukasoba": a ramen with mature chicken (chewy, firm texture) and a deep, rich soup. There is also a dipping version, the "Oya-dori Tsukesoba".

My take: I have eaten at the main shop. Even on weekdays there are people waiting in line. In Ebisu, it is one of the two most popular ramen shops, in my opinion. My recommendation is the standard chicken ramen. You can choose the thickness of the noodles. The thickest noodles are chewy like rice cake (mochi) and quite heavy and stiff. They are an experience even for Japanese people, so first-time visitors will probably be surprised. (The names of the noodle options differ between sources, so check the menu at the shop.)

4. Ramen Kanmuro (chicken paitan)

Address: 1-7-11 Ebisu-nishi, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo
Getting there: About 2 to 3 minutes on foot from the West Exit of Ebisu Station, near Ebisu Shrine.
Hours: Mon to Thu and Sun: 11:30 to 22:00. Fri: 11:30 to 4:00 the next morning. Sat: 11:30 to 22:30. (Check before you go.)
Menu: The signature bowl is the chicken paitan ramen: a rich, creamy chicken soup. There is also tsukemen (dipping noodles).

My take: I visited on October 21, 2026. The ramen is very refined. The taste is strong, and it is very good. The chicken paitan ramen is a very satisfying bowl, as you would expect from a popular shop.

Which one should you choose?

  • Something unusual, with free bean sprouts and green onions: Tsukumo
  • A huge, powerful bowl: Butayama
  • Broth and noodles you can taste carefully: Ayagawa
  • Creamy chicken soup: Kanmuro

Prices are not listed here because they change. Many ramen shops in Japan use a ticket machine: pay first, then hand the ticket to the staff. Some popular shops have a line at lunch and dinner time.
