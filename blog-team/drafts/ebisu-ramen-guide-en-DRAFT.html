What kind of neighborhood is Ebisu?

Ebisu is a relaxed, grown-up neighborhood just south of Shibuya. It is popular with people who live in Tokyo, including many from overseas, and it has a lot of small restaurants and bars.

  • Getting there: It is one stop from Shibuya on the JR Yamanote Line. From Shinjuku, the JR Saikyo Line is a direct option. The Hibiya Line also stops here. Check a route planner for today's times and fares.
  • Yebisu Garden Place is the area's big shopping and dining complex. The neighborhood is named after Yebisu Beer, which was brewed on this site.
  • Ebisu Yokocho is a lively alley of casual izakaya (Japanese pubs).

It is also a very good ramen neighborhood. Here are four shops, each with a different style.

1. Tsukumo Ramen, Ebisu Main Shop (cheese ramen)

Address: 1-1-36 Hiroo, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (Hongoku Building, 1st floor)
Getting there: About 5 to 7 minutes on foot from the West Exit of Ebisu Station (sources differ slightly).
Hours: Open late into the night. Sources give different closing times, so check before you go.
Signature bowl: the "Ganso Maruku Cheese Ramen", topped with a pile of cheese. The shop says the cheese comes from Hokkaido.
Ordering: You buy a ticket from a machine outside, and you can choose things like noodle thickness and the amount of back fat.

3. Teuchi Oya-dori Chukasoba Ayagawa (chicken broth, hand-cut noodles)

Address: 1-21-18 Ebisu, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (Lights Ebisu building, 1st floor)
Getting there: About 4 minutes on foot from Ebisu Station (JR Yamanote, Saikyo and Hibiya lines).
Hours: Listed as 11:00 to 21:30, open every day. It may close early when the noodles sell out.
Style: A soy-sauce ramen with a broth made from mature chicken ("oya-dori") and thick hand-cut noodles.

Which one should you choose?

  • Something unusual to show your friends: Tsukumo
  • A huge, powerful bowl: Butayama
  • Broth and noodles you can taste carefully: Ayagawa
  • Creamy chicken soup: Kanmuri-o
