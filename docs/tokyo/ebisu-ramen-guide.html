Ebisu Ramen Guide: 4 Very Different Bowls Near the Station

Written October 2026. I visited and ate at all four shops. Addresses, hours and walking times come from my own notes and from public guides, checked on October 21, 2026. They change, so check each shop's latest information before you go. This is an unofficial guide and is not connected to any of the shops.

Ebisu is a famous ramen battleground, with dozens of ramen shops. In my view, the four shops in this guide are among the most popular in the area, and each one has a very different style.

What kind of neighborhood is Ebisu?

Ebisu is a relaxed, grown-up neighborhood just south of Shibuya. It is popular with people who live in Tokyo, including many from overseas, and it has a lot of small restaurants and bars.

  • Getting there: It is one stop from Shibuya on the JR Yamanote Line. From Shinjuku, the JR Saikyo Line is a direct option. The Hibiya Line also stops here. Check a route planner for today's times and fares.
  • Yebisu Garden Place is the area's big shopping and dining complex. The neighborhood is named after Yebisu Beer, which was brewed on this site.
  • Ebisu Yokocho is a lively alley of casual izakaya (Japanese pubs).

Now, on to the ramen.

1. Tsukumo Ramen, Ebisu Main Shop (cheese ramen)

九十九ラーメン 恵比寿本店 · Open in Google Maps

Address: 1-1-36 Hiroo, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (Hongoku Building, 1st floor)
Getting there: About 7 to 8 minutes on foot from the East Exit of Ebisu Station, going straight along Meiji-dori toward Hiroo. Walking times differ by source, so use a map app.
Hours: 11:00 to 3:00 the next morning. Guides say it is open every day. Check before you go.
Signature bowl: the "Ganso (Original) Cheese Ramen", topped with a mountain of Gouda cheese from Tokachi, Hokkaido. The shop also serves the "Ganso Tsukumo Tororo Ramen".
Ordering: According to published guides, you buy a ticket from a machine at the entrance, and you can choose things like noodle thickness and the amount of back fat.

My take: I have eaten here. The cheese is piled high, and the bowl is satisfying. Free bean sprouts and green onions are on every table. Add as much as you like, and make the bowl your own. Visitors from overseas will be surprised. There is nothing I would change about this shop.

Cheese is a dairy product. If you avoid dairy, ask the staff first.

2. Ramen Butayama, Ebisu (Jiro-style)

ラーメン豚山 恵比寿店 · Open in Google Maps

Address: 4-1-18 Ebisu, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo
Getting there: About 2 to 3 minutes on foot from the East Exit of Ebisu Station.
Hours: 11:00 to 2:00 the next morning. Closed days: not confirmed, so check before you go.
Payment: Cash, or a transit IC card such as Suica or PASMO. Credit cards are not accepted.
Style: "Jiro-style" ramen: thick noodles, a rich pork soup, thick-cut pork (chashu) and a lot of vegetables. There are several sizes, and the smallest is called "Small Ramen".

How to order (and the "Zen-mashi" bowl)

Just before your ramen is served, the staff will ask "Ninniku iremasu ka?" ("Do you want garlic?"). This is when you tell them how you want your bowl. The shop's own guide on the wall explains the options:

  • Garlic (ninniku): none (nuki), a little (sukoshi, half a tablespoon), regular (1 tablespoon) or extra (mashimashi, 2 tablespoons).
  • Vegetables (yasai): less (150 g), standard (300 g), more (450 g) or extra (600 g). The shop says the 600 g option is very large and asks first-time visitors to avoid it.
  • Back fat (abura; fat from the pork back, added on top of the vegetables): standard, more (abura) or extra (abura mashimashi).
  • Strength of the sauce: standard, stronger (karame) or very strong (karakara).
  • If you are happy with the standard toppings, you do not need to say anything. The guide recommends the standard size for first-timers.

The shop's guide also lists "Zen-mashi" (全マシ): garlic, vegetables at 450 g, more back fat (abura) and a stronger sauce (karame). That is the bowl in the photo below. The toppings are free. If you are confident that you can finish a big bowl, try it once. If not, order the standard bowl.

My take: I have eaten here, and I think most visitors have never had a bowl like this. It may be a real shock. Be careful when you order: even a small bowl is as big as a large bowl at an ordinary ramen shop.

3. Teuchi Oya-dori Chukasoba Ayagawa (chicken broth, hand-cut noodles)

手打 親鶏中華そば 綾川 · Open in Google Maps

Address: 1-21-18 Ebisu, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo (Lights Ebisu building, 1st floor)
Getting there: About 5 to 6 minutes on foot from the East Exit of Ebisu Station.
Hours: 11:00 to 21:30. Guides say it is open every day. It may close early when the noodles sell out.
Ordering: Buy a ticket at the machine (it has English, Chinese and Korean modes, and an electronic money option). You choose medium-thick or extra-thick noodles on the machine.
Style: The signature bowl is the "Oya-dori Chukasoba": a ramen with mature chicken (chewy, firm texture) and a deep, rich soup. According to guides, there is also a dipping version, the "Oya-dori Tsukesoba".

My take: I have eaten at the main shop. Even on weekdays there are people waiting in line. In my opinion, it is one of the most popular ramen shops in Ebisu. My recommendation is the standard chicken ramen (Oya-dori Chukasoba). The extra-thick noodles are chewy like rice cake (mochi) and quite heavy and stiff. They are a strong experience even for Japanese people, so I think first-time visitors will be surprised.

4. Ramen Kamuro (chicken paitan)

らぁめん冠尾(かむろ) · Open in Google Maps

Address: 1-7-11 Ebisu-nishi, Shibuya-ku, Tokyo
Getting there: About 2 to 3 minutes on foot from the West Exit of Ebisu Station, near Ebisu Shrine.
Hours: Mon to Thu and Sun: 11:30 to 22:00. Fri: 11:30 to 4:00 the next morning. Sat: 11:30 to 22:30. Closed days: not confirmed. Check before you go.
Menu: The signature bowl is the chicken paitan ramen: a rich, creamy chicken soup. There is also tsukemen (dipping noodles).

My take: I visited on October 21, 2026. The ramen is refined. The taste is rich, and it is delicious. The chicken paitan ramen is a satisfying bowl, as you would expect from a popular shop.

Which one should you choose?

  • Something unusual, with free bean sprouts and green onions: Tsukumo
  • A huge, powerful bowl: Butayama
  • Broth and noodles you can taste carefully: Ayagawa
  • Creamy chicken soup: Kamuro

The soups at these shops are meat-based (chicken or pork), and the noodles contain wheat. If you have allergies or follow a vegetarian or halal diet, ask the staff or choose another shop. Payment methods differ by shop, so ask at the counter. Prices are not listed here because they change. Many ramen shops in Japan use a ticket machine: pay first, then hand the ticket to the staff. Some popular shops have a line at lunch and dinner time.
